What each one is

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. Every tenant in #4471 writes the day first, so any date with a day of 12 or less comes out swapped: 7 April becomes 4 July. This is what #4471 says it is. Fixed.

hint: booking-v3 tells day-first people to type month first. The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. Its tenants’ people follow the hint, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The symptom is the same swap, so the subject lines match. The fix is in the form, not the parser. Only reports through the form channel of a booking-v3 tenant are this one. Intake’s hint tag guesses from the reporter’s browser, so do not trust it for this. Won’t fix: the booking-v3 form is retired, so no new reports can come this way.

shift: the date moves by one day. No day and month swap: “12 May shows 11 May”, an invite that arrives before the booking was made, a client in Lisbon who got Tuesday while a client in Toronto got Monday. That looks like a time zone or date-boundary bug. The desk had already marked these as a one-day move in its notes, but the #4471 page still files them under the parser. Still open.

Lessons

  • Same subject is not same cause. Before you mark a big ticket known, place its reports by tenant and channel (causes.ts does this), and read the ones that do not fit.
  • A note that a report “reads differently” is a sign of a second problem. Give it a ticket, not a footnote.
